# Readiness follows decisions, not phase order

The Product → Frontend → Architecture → Implementation sequence is the usual
feature route. The advisor may recommend a decision earlier when its necessary
accepted inputs suffice and remaining open choices cannot invalidate it. It
reports that basis separately from whole-phase completeness. More than one
decision may be ready; advice selects one and explains useful alternatives.

This supersedes
[ADR-0009](0009-every-proposed-artifact-keeps-its-phase-open.md). Initial
foundation work still needs accepted catalog coverage; adding a later proposal
does not withdraw accepted decisions or send every feature back to foundation.
A draft replacement identifies its target passage and leaves the current rule
in force until agreed. An agreed change requires review of affected decisions
and transitive dependants, not indiscriminate reopening by directory location.

## Consequences

Folders and marker lines become the start of a reading, not proof of phase
state. Completion requires accepted, compatible answers or dismissals for every
catalog question and no unresolved input needed by that coverage. A folder of
accepted artifacts may be incomplete; a complete phase may also contain an
unrelated proposed extension. The advisor reads dependencies, relevant decision
passages, and the catalog before making either claim.

An export file contract can be ready while its launch screen is unresolved if
accepted content and export rules establish the contract independently of that
screen. If delivery or failure behavior still depends on the interaction
choice, the advisor names that missing decision instead. Speculative work at
the user's request stays proposed. Cyclic inputs are resolved together and
cannot establish each other's readiness merely by linking drafts.

The simpler marker-only rule was cheap to inspect but treated new proposals as
withdrawal of current decisions and made independent work appear premature.
The chosen semantic reading costs more and can be wrong; advice therefore names
the supporting passages and discloses coverage it has not verified.
